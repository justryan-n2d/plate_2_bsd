## 1. Project goal

Develop a complete, coordinated **preliminary building design** for a proposed two-storey residence. The design must respond to the assignment brief, the supplied lot description, the needs of 5 to 6 occupants, applicable code requirements, functional space planning, and the required printed drawing and documentation package.

The work must be traceable: key dimensions, areas, setback decisions, design choices, calculations, and code checks should have a recorded basis. Do not hide unresolved information behind guessed dimensions.

### Definition of done

- [ ] Every required drawing, calculation, schedule, and written document in the assignment is accounted for.
- [ ] The lot description has been checked for arithmetic closure and consistency before it is used as a final site base.
- [ ] Road right-of-way, setbacks, buildable envelope, and local requirements have a documented basis.
- [ ] Ground-floor and second-floor plans satisfy the space list and have coordinated dimensions, doors, windows, stairs, furniture, and circulation.
- [ ] Roof, elevations, sections, schedules, and details agree with the floor plans.
- [ ] Area, stair, parking, occupant-load, and other required calculations are shown and independently checked.
- [ ] Preliminary specifications match the materials and finishes actually shown.
- [ ] Scope questions, especially structural drawings listed in the submission checklist, have been clarified with the instructor.
- [ ] All sheets use the required sheet size, title-block information, units, scales, labels, and symbols.
- [ ] The final files are readable, organized, versioned, and ready for the instructor's review.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 Included in this project

**Site and planning**
- Interpret the technical description and produce a verified site base plan.
- Plot property boundaries, bearings, distances, north direction, road frontage, and adjacent properties.
- Determine applicable setbacks, open-space requirements, buildable envelope, building footprint, parking requirement, and access arrangement.
- Analyze solar orientation, pedestrian access, vehicular access, service access, and existing site conditions.
- Prepare a space-requirement table and adjacency/relationship diagram.

**Preliminary architecture**
- Ground floor: entrance/foyer, living room, dining area, kitchen, toilet and bath, bedroom/guest room, stairway, storage/service area, and garage/carport.
- Second floor: master bedroom, Bedrooms 2 and 3, toilet and bath, family area, stair landing/circulation, and balcony if appropriate.
- Roof plan, four elevations, and at least two building sections, including at least one section through the stairway.
- Architectural details and schedules listed in the assignment.
- Preliminary building specifications, calculations, code-compliance checklist, and design rationale.

**Coordination**
- Keep drawing dimensions, area calculations, schedules, and specifications consistent.
- Consider safe circulation, privacy, ventilation, daylight, access, stairs, and applicable building/fire-safety requirements within the architectural design scope.
- Preserve a record of source information, assumptions, decisions, and checks.

### 3.2 Excluded or limited by the assignment

The project description says detailed utility systems are **not part of Plate No. 2**. Do not independently expand this plate into full designs for:
- Plumbing and sanitary systems
- Electrical systems
- Mechanical systems
- Fire-protection utility systems
- Other detailed building services

Architectural code considerations that affect the layout still need to be checked; excluding detailed utility design does not mean ignoring life safety.

### 3.3 Important scope conflict: structural drawings

The narrative says this plate is limited to the building itself and describes the approved preliminary plan as the basis for later structural and utility design. However, the required-submission checklist also lists:
- Column, footing, and beam details and schedules
- Foundation plan
- Second-floor framing plan

**Do not silently ignore this list, and do not invent structural sizes or reinforcement details.** Ask the instructor whether these items are required now, are preliminary/conceptual only, or belong to a succeeding plate. Record the answer in the project decision log. If they are required, request the expected calculation depth, design assumptions, applicable structural code/edition, and required level of detail.

### 3.4 What this project is not

This is an academic preliminary design, not a claim that the building is permit-ready, construction-ready, structurally certified, or compliant with every requirement. The project does not replace the review of the instructor, local authorities, licensed design professionals, a verified survey, or a geotechnical investigation when those are required.

## 4. High-priority issues to resolve before design

Do these before finalizing setbacks or committing to the building footprint.

### BLOCKER-01: Traverse closure appears inconsistent

A preliminary component check of the five bearings and distances in the assignment gives approximately:
- Easting misclosure: **-3.984 m**
- Northing misclosure: **+0.476 m**
- Linear misclosure: **4.012 m**
- Traverse perimeter from listed courses: **64.70 m**

This is a large misclosure for a small lot and should not be treated as a harmless rounding difference. Independently recalculate the bearings, quadrant signs, coordinate increments, and closure from the original brief. Then ask the instructor to confirm whether the bearing/distance entries contain a transcription error or whether a corrected survey description is available.

**Rule:** Do not force the traverse to close, adjust the bearings, or silently change the distances. Keep the supplied values and the check calculation in the record until corrected source information is authorized.

### BLOCKER-02: Road width and right-of-way

The project-information table specifies an 8.00 m road right-of-way, while the lot boundary description refers to an existing 5.0 m barangay road. Confirm:
- Whether 5.0 m describes the present road pavement, the traveled way, or the legal right-of-way.
- Whether the official road right-of-way is 8.00 m.
- The official road/property boundary line used to measure setbacks.
- Which lot sides actually front the road and where vehicle access is permitted.

Do not set the driveway, carport, building line, or setbacks based only on an assumed road edge.

### BLOCKER-03: Stated area versus computed area

The lot is described as approximately 300 m². After the traverse data is confirmed and closed, calculate the area from coordinates and compare it with 300 m². Report any difference and its percentage. Do not change the stated area or geometry simply to make the values agree.

### BLOCKER-04: Survey-plan reference

Confirm whether `Psd-08-2026-XXX` is the exact classroom data or a placeholder. Use only instructor-approved survey information in the final site plan.

### BLOCKER-05: Structural submission scope

Get written or otherwise traceable clarification from the instructor on the structural drawings listed in Section XVI of the brief. Track the answer before structural sheets are prepared.

### BLOCKER-06: Applicable local requirements

Confirm the current zoning/land-use classification for the stated property, local building requirements, legal setbacks, easements, allowed residential use, parking, and road-access conditions. If official local information is unavailable, record that limitation and ask the instructor how to proceed. Do not claim verified local compliance based only on a generic online source.

### Other decisions to record

- Exact north orientation and whether a survey plan includes coordinates or a north reference.
- Required drawing scales and preferred CAD/manual conventions.
- School name, student name, section, instructor, date, and any required title-block template.
- Any instructor-approved assumptions where site or user information is missing.

## 10. Design and quality principles

1. **Verified data before detailed drawing.** Resolve the lot geometry and road reference before fixing the footprint.
2. **Code dimensions need sources.** Every important regulated dimension should be tied to the applicable code/local source or marked for instructor confirmation.
3. **Plan the spaces before detailing.** Use the space table and adjacency diagram to explain room relationships.
4. **One coordinated model of dimensions.** A dimension should not change between a floor plan, section, elevation, calculation, and schedule without an explicit revision.
5. **Do not fabricate missing facts.** Unknown survey information, road boundaries, soil parameters, code applicability, and structural data must remain visible as questions or limitations.
6. **Keep utility scope controlled.** Detailed utility systems belong to later plates unless the instructor explicitly adds them here.
7. **Check legibility at print size.** A drawing is not finished merely because it looks clear when zoomed in on screen.
8. **Keep the project reviewable.** Save source files, calculations, official references, decisions, exports, and a final checklist.
